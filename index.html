A never-ending well of curiosity.

Before the workshop, before the first drawing, there was a pull.

Something turned inward and opened at the same moment. It has not stopped turning.

Every form made here is drawn up from it. Every form goes back.

The well is not explained. It is visited.

Practice / BorderlandsText — Joel Junsjö

Work at the edge of what a form can hold.

My work sits in the borderlands between art, science and building. I make physical forms to think with, about existence, consciousness and evolution.

For many years I have built worlds for others: sets, rooms, special builds, surfaces that look like something they are not. That craft taught me how material behaves and how people read space. NPF turns that knowledge toward my own questions.

The questions are old. How does something come to exist rather than nothing? How does matter become aware of itself? How do patterns repeat, branch and change over time? I do not answer them in words. I build models: vortices, tori, spines and rings that copy the structures nature keeps returning to, and I study what happens when they move, catch light, or fail.

This is research in the maker's sense. The workshop is the laboratory, and the object is the argument.

Axis 01

Existence

Why form appears at all. The vortex as the simplest shape that holds itself together while moving.

Axis 02

Consciousness

Inside and outside of the same surface. The torus as a model of a system that folds back on itself.

Axis 03

Evolution

Repetition, branching and drift. Forms that grow by copying themselves with small errors.

Method — not a division
Step 1

Build

Start with the object. A rough model reveals more than a finished sketch.

Step 2

Research

Measure, read and test what the model shows. Bring in maths, nature and other disciplines.

Step 3

Build

Build again with what was learned. Every finished piece is the start of the next question.
